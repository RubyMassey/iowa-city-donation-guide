Where to Donate Books in Iowa City

Finished reading? Give your books a new chapter by exploring ways to share them with readers in the Iowa City community.

Book Donation Options in the Iowa City Area

Books can have long lives beyond their first owners. Whether you have a few novels or several boxes of reading materials, there are different ways to make them available to others.

Community Book Exchanges

Small neighborhood book exchanges allow residents to leave books and discover something new to read. They can be a convenient option for donating a few books at a time.

Best for: Novels, children's books, and general-interest reading materials.

Library-Associated Book Sales

Some library support organizations organize book sales using donated materials. These events can help books reach new readers while supporting library-related activities.

Best for: Books in good condition that meet the organization's current acceptance guidelines.

Thrift Stores and Secondhand Shops

Secondhand stores may accept books alongside other household donations. This can be a practical option when you are clearing out several categories of belongings at once.

Best for: General fiction, nonfiction, cookbooks, and other reusable titles.

Student and Community Sharing Groups

Students and residents can sometimes exchange books directly through campus groups or neighborhood sharing networks. This approach may be useful for textbooks and specialized reading materials.

Best for: Course-related books, hobby guides, and titles that appeal to a particular audience.

What Types of Books Can You Donate?

  • Fiction: Novels, mysteries, fantasy, and other recreational reading.
  • Nonfiction: Biographies, history, travel, and general educational books.
  • Children's Books: Picture books, early readers, and chapter books.
  • Cookbooks: Recipe collections and cooking guides.
  • Textbooks: Recent editions that are still relevant to students.

Organizations may decline outdated reference books, damaged materials, or titles that are unlikely to be reused. Always confirm their preferences before donating.

How to Prepare Books for Donation

  1. Inspect condition: Look for water damage, mold, missing pages, or broken bindings.
  2. Remove personal items: Check for bookmarks, notes, receipts, and personal information.
  3. Sort by category: Separate children's books, novels, nonfiction, and textbooks when possible.
  4. Pack in small boxes: Books are heavy, so avoid overfilling containers.
  5. Check acceptance rules: Confirm that your chosen recipient currently accepts your book types and quantities.

A Tip for University of Iowa Students

Before donating old textbooks, check whether they are still used in current courses. If they are, sharing them directly with another student may be more useful than placing them in a general donation collection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I donate old textbooks?

Possibly. Recent editions may be useful to students, but outdated textbooks can be difficult to redistribute. Consider student exchange groups first.

Do libraries accept every donated book?

No. Library systems and their support organizations may have specific donation policies. Some accept books only during certain periods or through designated programs.

What should I do with damaged books?

Books with mold or serious water damage generally should not be donated. Check local recycling guidelines to determine whether other damaged books can be recycled.
